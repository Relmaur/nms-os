# Pyrite

## Quick Facts

- **Category:** Earth
- **Type:** Localised Earth Element
- **Rarity:** Uncommon
- **Used for:** Building, Crafting, Charging, Upgrading
- **Symbol:** Py
- **Value:** 62
- **Added in:** WorldsTwo

**Pyrite** is a resource.

## Summary

**Pyrite** (**Py**) is a [[resource]] and one of the [[Localised Earth Element|localised]] [[earth elements]]. It is used as fuel for the [[starship]]'s [[Pulse Engine]].

## Game description

Local mineral extract, typically found in large deposits or extracted from common materials after inspection with an Analysis Visor.

Typically found on planets with a desert environment.

## Source

- As a secondary element in some [[mineral|minerals]] or in [[Resource Deposit|Resource Deposits]] or [[Hotspot|Hotspots]] on planets with Barren [[biome|biomes]].
- Also available from some [[Galactic Trade Terminal|Galactic Trade Terminals]].

- **Refiner** · *Metal Restructuring* · 0.24 s per unit: 1 × [[Gold]] → 1 × Pyrite
- **Refiner** · *Mineral Alchemy* · 0.36 s per unit: 1 × [[Gold]] + 1 × [[Oxygen]] → 2 × Pyrite
- **Refiner** · *Floral Titration* · 0.36 s per unit: 2 × [[Cactus Flesh]] + 1 × [[Salt]] → 1 × Pyrite
- **Refiner** · *Environmental Element Transfer* · 0.36 s per unit: 2 × [[Uranium]] + 1 × [[Ferrite Dust]] → 1 × Pyrite
- **Refiner** · *Environmental Element Transfer* · 0.36 s per unit: 2 × [[Uranium]] + 1 × [[Pure Ferrite]] → 1 × Pyrite
- **Refiner** · *Loosen Bonds* · 0.36 s per unit: 1 × [[Lithium]] + 1 × [[Ferrite Dust]] → 1 × Pyrite
- **Refiner** · *Loosen Bonds* · 0.18 s per unit: 1 × [[Lithium]] + 1 × [[Pure Ferrite]] → 2 × Pyrite
- **Refiner** · *Loosen Bonds* · 0.12 s per unit: 1 × [[Lithium]] + 1 × [[Magnetised Ferrite]] → 3 × Pyrite

## Trivia

- Pyrite (FeS2, name derived from the Greek word pyrites, meaning "of fire") is an iron-sulfide, commonly mistaken for gold and thus nicknamed 'fool's gold'. In real life, Pyrite's closest use as fuel was previously a source of ignition in early firearms during the 16th-17th century but is also now commonly more used as a cathode material in Energizer's lithium batteries.
