## Summary

**Parasitica bulbifera** is found on [[Terra Lorrainae I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. It employs parasitic leaching to draw nutrients from neighboring flora and stores resources in semi‑liquid bulbs.

## Appearance

A multi-stemmed terrestrial flora consisting of several erect, vertically oriented stalks of pale tan to light brown coloration with a distinct braided or rope-like surface texture formed by interwoven fibrous ridges. The stalks support large, broad, lanceolate leaves of deep teal-green to dark gray-green hue, each displaying prominent parallel venation, slightly undulate margins, and occasional apical browning. The leaves are arranged in loose, alternating spirals or whorls along the upper portions of the stems, with smaller secondary shoots and reduced foliage emerging from the basal region. The overall morphology indicates an upright, herbaceous to semi-woody growth habit specialized for vertical elongation and maximal photosynthetic surface exposure.
