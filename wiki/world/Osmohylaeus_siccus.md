## Summary

**Osmohylaeus siccus** is a flora species found on [[Terra Lorrainae I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. It survives arid conditions through osmotic absorption via its hidden root system.

## Appearance

A sessile, radially symmetric flora consisting of a compact central body from which radiate twelve thick, elongated, lanceolate lobes arranged in a tight, star-like whorl. Each lobe tapers gradually to a rounded apex and exhibits a heavily striated, furrowed integument with a mottled olive-green to deep brownish surface texture suggestive of a tough, woody or waxy cuticle and prominent linear grain patterns. The lobes are dorsoventrally flattened proximally, becoming more cylindrical and robust toward the base where they fuse into the central mass. Overall morphology indicates a low-profile, defensive autotrophic organism specialized for maximal surface area exposure in a stationary, ground-hugging growth habit.

## Additional information

- The hidden root system allows access to deep soil moisture unavailable to surface-rooted competitors, a key adaptation to arid conditions.
- Its radially symmetric, ground-hugging morphology maximizes surface area for both dew collection and osmotic absorption while minimizing exposure to desiccating winds.
- The tough, waxy cuticle evident in the striated surface texture reduces water loss through transpiration, complementing its osmotic nutrient strategy.
