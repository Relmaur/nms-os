## Summary

**Geovivium cordis** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. This mineral is classified as a living stone with a dynamic liquid center.

### Appearance

A dense, fan-shaped mineral formation with a broad, slightly curved profile that resembles a partially opened bivalve shell or a vertical wedge. Its surface displays a striking, irregular pattern of dark greenish-gray to charcoal-black base color interlaced with bold, branching veins and patches of bright golden-tan to ochre, giving it a marbled or fossilized appearance. The texture is rough and layered, with visible horizontal banding and fine cracking throughout. The overall shape is asymmetrical, thicker at the base and flaring outward toward the top with a gently scalloped edge. No external lattice, rings, or protrusions are present. Overall morphology indicates a compact, living-stone mineral with a liquid center and high structural integrity.

## Additional information

### Notes

- The liquid center is believed to be a mineral-rich slurry that circulates slowly within the stone, contributing to its “living” classification.
- The branching golden veins may be composed of a distinct mineral phase, possibly indicating differential crystallization.
- Despite its high metal content, the stone retains a surprisingly compact structure with no visible external porosity.
