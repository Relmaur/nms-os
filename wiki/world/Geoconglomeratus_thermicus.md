## Summary

**Geoconglomeratus thermicus** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. This mineral is a soil amalgamation that functions as a powerful natural heat source.

### Appearance

A large, irregular mineral formation with a flattened, platform-like base and a rugged, multi‑layered upper structure. The main body consists of dark, heavily textured rock with a cracked and vesicular surface in shades of deep charcoal and brownish‑black. Embedded within and protruding from the rock are several sharp, angular, translucent orange‑to‑red crystals of varying sizes, concentrated mainly on the upper surfaces and sides. The overall shape is asymmetrical and blocky, resembling a natural outcrop or amalgamated boulder. Small glowing teal points are visible near the base of some crystal formations. Overall morphology indicates a dense, soil‑amalgamated mineral with a powerful internal heat source.
