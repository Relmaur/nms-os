## Summary

**Aerometallum pallens** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. This mineral formed from atmospheric metal condensation and exhibits a faint, self‑sustaining luminescence.

### Appearance

A large, irregularly shaped mineral mass with a roughly triangular to teardrop profile and a blunt, rounded apex. Its surface is dark olive‑green to brownish‑gray with a rough, layered texture marked by prominent horizontal striations, shallow grooves, and fine cracking patterns. Subtle metallic sheen is visible along some of the ridges and fracture lines, consistent with the high metal content. The overall form is compact and dense, with no external lattice, rings, or protrusions. Overall morphology indicates a dense atmospheric‑metal mineral with faint luminosity.

## Additional information

### Notes

- The mineral formed through direct condensation of metallic vapors in the upper atmosphere, later depositing on the surface.
- The faint luminescence is likely due to trace amounts of radioactive isotopes or persistent phosphorescence from crystallographic defects.
- Horizontal striations and layering suggest successive deposition events, recording atmospheric conditions over time.
