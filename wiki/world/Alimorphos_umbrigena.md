## Summary

**Alimorphos umbrigena** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. This regenerating flora has stunted roots and relies on external maintenance for nutrients; it is notable for its umbral roots and shadow‑adapted growth.

### Appearance

A tall, slender, multi‑limbed flora with a distinctly upright growth habit. It features a narrow, elongated central stalk of mottled tan and brown coloration with a porous, honeycomb‑like texture. From the upper portion of the stalk emerges a dense cluster of long, flexible, tentacle‑like appendages that curve and droop downward; these appendages are a dull grayish‑blue with a wrinkled, leathery surface and terminate in fine, hair‑like tips. The base divides into three long, root‑like legs that splay outward for stability, each ending in small, clawed root structures. The overall morphology suggests a highly mobile or semi‑sessile osmotrophic flora with adaptations for nutrient absorption in a dynamic environment.

## Additional information

### Notes

- Despite its stunted root structure, the plant is capable of regenerating damaged tissues, allowing it to persist in harsh conditions.
- The umbral roots may absorb nutrients through symbiotic relationships with underground organisms or via passive uptake from decaying organic matter.
- Its preference for shadowed environments suggests an adaptation to low‑light conditions, possibly utilizing alternative energy sources.
