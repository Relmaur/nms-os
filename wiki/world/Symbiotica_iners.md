**Symbiotica iners** is a species of flora.

## Summary

**Symbiotica iners** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. It is a seasoned, symbiotic plant that draws nutrients through osmosis and is noted for its biological inertness.

### Appearance

The subject is a low-growing, multi-lobed flora consisting of a broad, irregular basal mass from which rise five prominent, upright, finger-like or columnar projections of varying heights. The central column is the tallest and most robust, while the surrounding lobes are shorter and more rounded. The entire organism displays a striking bicolored pattern: the lower portions and basal mass are a deep terracotta-red to orange-brown with a cracked, dry surface texture, while the upper halves of the columns are covered in a thick, glossy, deep purple to violet crust with a heavily fissured and blistered appearance. The transition between the two colors is abrupt and irregular. Overall morphology indicates a sessile, symbiotic or osmotrophic flora with a hardened, protective outer layer.

## Additional information

### Notes

- Despite its symbiotic root structure, the plant is biologically inert—it does not actively react to stimuli or engage in metabolic processes detectable by standard scanners.
- The thick, crusty upper surface may serve as a protective barrier against the planet’s toxic atmosphere.
- It is believed to draw nutrients through passive osmosis, relying on external fungal networks or mineral solutions in the soil.
