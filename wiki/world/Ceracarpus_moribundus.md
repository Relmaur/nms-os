**Ceracarpus moribundus** is a species of flora.

## Summary

**Ceracarpus moribundus** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. This dying flora relies on photosynthesis but possesses heat‑seeking roots to access geothermal warmth, and its waxy fruit persist even as the plant declines.

### Appearance

A tall, woody plant with a central stalk that shows clear signs of decline: the bark is cracked and peeling, and the upper branches are bare. The stalk is a pale, desiccated gray‑brown, while the few remaining branches near the crown are tipped with clusters of waxy, pale‑yellow fruit that have a smooth, almost translucent surface. The fruit hang like small lanterns, seemingly unaffected by the plant’s overall decay. The root system is extensive but not visible; thermal scans indicate it reaches deep into the substrate, following geothermal gradients. The overall morphology suggests a senescent but persistent flora whose last reproductive effort is preserved in its long‑lasting, waxy seed pods.
