## Summary

**Thermosentiens aquatilis** is found on [[Terra Lorrainae I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. It recycles ambient heat for energy and possesses sentient roots that seek geothermal veins while remaining water‑resistant.

## Appearance

A tall, vertically stacked floral organism composed of four to five large, ovoid nodules arranged in a helical, interlocking spiral that forms an unstable-looking column. Each nodule is roughly spherical to ellipsoidal, with a thick, cracked, terracotta-orange to reddish-brown exocarp exhibiting a deeply fissured, desiccated surface texture resembling dried clay or baked earth, marked by irregular polygonal fractures and subtle radial cracking. The nodules decrease slightly in size from base to apex, with the lowest providing the broadest foundation and the uppermost capped by a smaller, slightly elongated segment. No visible leaves, stems, or reproductive structures project externally; the overall form suggests a highly derived, heat-tolerant autotroph with adaptations for long-term structural stability and minimal surface exposure.

## Additional information

- The sentient root network actively navigates toward geothermal veins, allowing the plant to harvest subterranean heat even in waterlogged or saturated soils.
- Its water‑resistant exocarp prevents moisture from leaching heat from the nodules, maintaining internal temperatures optimal for heat‑recycling metabolism.
- The columnar, spiral arrangement minimizes surface area exposed to cooling winds while maximizing structural stability over centuries of growth.
