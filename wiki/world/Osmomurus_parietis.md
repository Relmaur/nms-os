## Summary

**Osmomurus parietis** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. This infant flora draws nutrients through osmosis and is noted for its “wailing roots” and limited root structure.

### Appearance

A tall, slender, multi-limbed flora with a distinctly bipedal or tripod-like stance. It features a narrow, elongated central stalk of mottled tan and brown coloration with a porous, honeycomb-like texture. From the upper portion of the stalk emerges a dense cluster of long, flexible, tentacle-like appendages that curve and droop downward; these appendages are a dull grayish-blue with a wrinkled, leathery surface and terminate in fine, hair-like tips. The base divides into three long, root-like legs that splay outward for stability, each ending in small, clawed root structures. The overall morphology suggests a highly mobile or semi-sessile osmotrophic flora with “wailing roots” adapted for nutrient absorption in a dynamic environment.

## Additional information

### Notes

- The “wailing roots” are believed to produce a low-frequency vibration or sound when absorbing moisture, possibly to deter herbivores or attract symbiotic organisms.
- Despite its infant stage, the plant already exhibits a complex root structure that aids in stability and nutrient uptake.
- The tentacle-like appendages may serve to increase surface area for moisture absorption in the planet’s toxic, humid environment.
