## Summary

**Fractaliflor rarus** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. This sapling‑stage flora uses a fractal root system for photosynthesis and is noted for its infrequent, irregular flowering cycle.

### Appearance

A tall, slender, multi‑limbed flora with a distinctly upright growth habit. It features a narrow, elongated central stalk of mottled tan and brown coloration with a porous, honeycomb‑like texture. From the upper portion of the stalk emerges a dense cluster of long, flexible, tentacle‑like appendages that curve and droop downward; these appendages are a dull grayish‑blue with a wrinkled, leathery surface and terminate in fine, hair‑like tips. The base divides into three long, root‑like legs that splay outward for stability, each ending in small, clawed root structures. The overall morphology suggests a highly mobile or semi‑sessile osmotrophic flora with fractal root structure and infrequent flowering.
