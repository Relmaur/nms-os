## Summary

**Gravicompactus carbonifer** is encountered on [[Sanctus Nathanaelis I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. This sprouting flora relies on gravitational orientation for root growth and membrane compression for nutrient synthesis, producing a carbon‑dense structure.

### Appearance

A low‑growing, multi‑lobed flora consisting of a broad, irregular basal mass from which rise several upright, finger‑like or columnar projections of varying heights. The tallest central column is robust and rounded at the apex, while the surrounding lobes are shorter and more bulbous. The coloration is sharply bicolored: the lower portions and basal mass are a vivid turquoise‑blue with a glossy, slightly blistered texture, while the upper halves of the columns are covered in a thick, yellowish‑green crust with a heavily cracked and porous surface. The transition between the two colors is irregular and abrupt. Overall morphology indicates a sessile, gravitationally adapted flora with a carbon‑dense, sprouting structure.
