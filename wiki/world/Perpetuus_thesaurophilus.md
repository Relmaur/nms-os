## Summary

**Perpetuus thesaurophilus** is a flora species found on [[Terra Lorrainae I]] in the [[Ioseph]] system. It draws sustenance from buried mineral deposits and is notable for its perpetually ticklish stamen.

## Appearance

The subject is a tall, solitary floral organism consisting of a thick, vertically oriented stem approximately cylindrical in cross-section and marked by irregular horizontal segmentation with a rough, woody integument of mottled dark green and brown tones. Surmounting the stem is a single massive terminal bloom forming an elongated, trumpet-shaped corolla that flares broadly upward into a wide, open cup; the corolla wall is distinctly pleated with prominent vertical ribs and displays a luminous pink-to-magenta inner surface that fades to darker brownish tones at the base, terminating in a delicate, scalloped white margin with undulate, frilled edges. A swollen, bulbous basal structure of overlapping brownish bracts anchors the bloom to the stem. Overall morphology indicates an erect, specialized floral structure optimized for elevated display.
